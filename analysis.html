HF Radar Spectral Analysis

← back to the simulation

Subtidal surface-current spectra decomposed into rotational (geostrophic-like) and divergent parts, for testing the slopes predicted by quasi-normal scale elimination (QNSE) and 2-D turbulence: k−5/3 (inverse energy cascade) versus k−3 (enstrophy cascade).

Method

  • Totals. NOAA NCEI CODAR radials are streamed from the archive (no raw files stored) and combined into total vectors by unweighted least squares on a regular grid. QC: VFLG = 0, |v| ≤ 1.5 m/s, ≥ 3 radials from ≥ 2 sites within the search radius, crossing angle ≥ 30° (GDOP). GISTDA totals are used on their native 2 km grid.
  • Subtidal filter. Lanczos low-pass, 72 h half-power, ±120 h window. It removes the tides and the near-inertial band (≈ 20 h at 37°N, ≈ 53 h at 13°N).
  • Spectra. 1-D longitudinal CL and transverse CT spectra along zonal and meridional transects (gap-tolerant, de-meaned, Tukey-tapered segments), split into rotational Dψ and divergent Dφ parts after Bühler, Callies & Ferrari (2014, J. Fluid Mech. 757).
  • Fits. Power laws are fitted only above each product's smoothing scale: 2.2 × the radial search radius for NCEI, 20 km for GISTDA.
  • Verdict. Each network's estimator is validated on synthetic months with its real gap mask. A slope is called consistent with a law when the bias-corrected difference is within 2σ, where σ combines the fit error and the validated scatter.
  • Caveats. The split assumes isotropy; a rotational share > 1 flags anisotropic flow. Footprints limit the largest resolved wavelength, so these data probe scales up to about the deformation radius, not the Rhines scale where QNSE's zonal anisotropy is predicted.
